## What just happened

The consumer started first and parked in `take()`, because the queue was empty. The producer put `a`, which woke the consumer. The producer then filled the queue with `b` and `c`. With no room left, `put(d)` parked the producer. As soon as the consumer took `b`, a slot opened and `d` went in.

## Try the what-if switch

**unbounded**: with `new LinkedBlockingQueue<>()`, `put()` never waits. The producer finishes at once and the jobs pile up in the queue. Nothing slows the producer down, so with a fast producer the queue grows until memory runs out.

## The queue is the contract

Neither side knows about the other. They only share the queue:

- `put()` waits when the queue is full: the producer slows down to the consumer's speed.
- `take()` waits when it's empty: the consumer rests until there is work.

That automatic slowing down is called **backpressure**.

## Pick the right queue

- `ArrayBlockingQueue(n)`: fixed size, backpressure built in. A good default.
- `LinkedBlockingQueue()`: no limit unless you pass one. Without a limit, `put()` never waits.
- `SynchronousQueue`: no storage at all. Each `put()` waits for a `take()`, a direct hand-off.

## Stopping cleanly

A common pattern is a special "poison pill" item: the producer puts a marker like `"STOP"` last, and the consumer exits when it takes it.

You've already used this pattern: a `ThreadPoolExecutor`'s threads are consumers taking tasks from a `BlockingQueue`.
